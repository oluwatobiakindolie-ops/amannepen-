01 Remove Your Beam First Sept 3, 2026 +

Why is it so easy for us to point out the flaws in other people without stopping to acknowledge our own? Gone are the days when I would read a quote about human behaviour and immediately nod in agreement, thinking about all the people it applies to. Now that I am grown and still growing, I have learnt to look inwardly and remove the beam in my own eyes first. There has been a recurring pattern about how easily we agree with certain opinions about human behaviour, relationships, character, and people in general. But we forget that sometimes we may be guilty of the very things we are condemning. What prompted me to publish this piece was an audiobook I was listening to in the early hours of today, titled Human Psychology. Everything I jotted down were bullet points, and while I was ruminating on those words and how I can apply it in my dealings with people, I had this nudge to pause the audio and read what I wrote again. And that was when something became clear to me. Right there, I understood that learning about human behaviour is not enough. If I am not careful, I can consume all that knowledge and use it to analyze, criticize, and explain other people's behaviour without ever examining my own.

So, before applying what I have learnt to the people around me, I had to ask myself some uncomfortable questions: ‘Ann, what if you possess some of the traits you're criticizing?’ ‘Okay, have I behaved this way toward someone else?’ ‘Am I expecting from people what I am unwilling to practise myself?’ The core message of this piece is that knowledge should not only help you understand other people, it should also help you confront yourself. Because honestly speaking, that's one of the real signs of growth, not the one that you quickly recognize what is wrong with others. The goal is not merely to know better, it is to become better. And you can only do that when you are willing to recognise what needs to change in you.

02 Refuse To Be Small Sept 4, 2026 +

I was in Basic 4 when I represented my class in a quiz competition. At that time, my elementary school organized extracurricular activities. Initially, I was skeptical about representing my class, but who dared go against their teacher's instructions? Each class was to present three representatives, and our opponent was the Basic 5 students. We were intimidated because we perceived those pupils to be naturally more knowledgeable than us. But guess what? We eventually did not win the quiz competition. We didn't win because we thought we were incapable of defeating them. Our teacher got so infuriated about the fact that we solved each mathematical question and got the correct answer, only to stand up and choose the wrong option. Can you imagine, we doubted our calculations and assumed we had made mistakes? It got worse, that other teachers had to motivate us to answer with confidence. In fact, we even argued among ourselves while sitting on the podium because none of us trusted our reasoning. The story is funny, but it had numerous lessons in it.

I was reminded of it after watching one of AFL's short videos where he said something that struck me deeply, and it immediately took me back to that day. Like my classmates and me, many people don't fail because they lack knowledge or ability. They fail because they have already accepted a story about themselves. Stories like they are too small, too inexperienced, too young, or not good enough. And allow me to give you a few points about smallness. Smallness is a belief that will shape every decision you make. It will make you second-guess the right answers you already have. It can silence your voice, even when you know what to say. The worst of it all is that, it creates division where there should be unity and fear where there should be confidence. That is why you have to be careful of the narrative you choose to believe about yourself, it can become your greatest obstacle. So, repeat after me: I refuse to be small. Everything about me reflects greatness. Now that you have declared it, go and act like it.

03 Danger of Information Overload Sept 5, 2026 +

One of the fastest ways to lose your creativity is through information overload. Information is valuable, but in this time and age, many people haven't unlocked their creative potential because of what they constantly consume. Instead of creating, they begin to replicate and imitate other people's ideas and innovation. The question is how do you unlock your creativity? The only answer is by spending time with yourself. Creativity doesn't grow in endless scrolling. It grows in moments of solitude, reflection, and self-awareness.

When you spend time with yourself, you begin to discover what you're naturally good at, what needs improvement, and how you uniquely express your ideas. For instance, you and I can be content creators but we don't do the same thing, or we both could talk about self-development but our methodology is so different. That is your experience, voice, perspectives, and ways of communicating is your creative advantage. Can you see that? If you want to unlock your full creativity, disconnect from the noise once in a while and reconnect with yourself. Your best ideas are waiting for you there.

04 Navigating Through Disappointment Sept 11, 2026 +

Dear twenty-something to thirty, let's have a real and honest conversation. How do you feel when life doesn't turn out the way you planned? Are you happy, sad or disappointed? I used that age bracket because there are young Nigerians youths who are likely to be an ex-corp member, corp member, graduate, or undergraduate. But there is this pressure that comes with this particular stage in life, and one question you're most likely to ask yourself is “What next?” especially if you don't have a solid back-up, or you come from a poor background. This thought alone could make you perform to your peers and family, fill you with doubt and uncertainty as the days go by, or even make you settle for less if care is not taken. The question is, why is it like that? System. We can say that the system is designed in such a way that if you don't wake up on time, there's every tendency that you will never utilize your potential because it's like your life was meant to follow a strict program. They told you that education will pave the way which often happens, but they never told you that going to school alone isn't enough. Exposure is also another form of knowledge. To you, who are reading this piece right now, deep down you wish you skill stacked. You wished you tried that business. You wished you tried out that passion.

But you never did all of these because of this system that was made for you not to think out of the box. A system that doesn't want you to be curious and inquisitive about why things are precisely the way they were. A system that can make a person settle for less, and you keep wondering ‘how did it go wrong?’ It went wrong when what is perceived to be a sign of ‘living well’ was passed down to you. A vivid example is what this post lays emphasis on—career. It's the dream of every learned young individual to secure a decent and high-paying job that reflects their qualifications and the current economy of the nation. Meanwhile, don't think your life is at a pause simply because you don't have that white-collar job. Life will not halt its operation because one person feels nothing is going on for them. You're only trading time meant for learning, preparation, and equipping yourself in other fields for drowning in self-pity and confusion.

And if you are currently going through this, here are 3 practical steps that will help you out of that circumstance. Firstly, remember that you're not alone. My mum usually makes a statement that really resonates with the first point: “Every one is in the middle”, and it's true. If you think you've seen or experienced it all, hear the story of another person. Secondly, ponder on your strengths and weaknesses—that is, what are those things you're good at. What are those things that you're partially good at, but with learning you would become an expert. You possibly can't know all of these unless you spend time with yourself. So, instead of overthinking your current situation and engaging in self-pity, the best you can do is to take charge of your thoughts. Lastly, take action. You can do this by skill stacking, exploring your passions, executing that idea you once watered down, show the world what you've got, and place a price on what you can offer. Your life doesn't have to be on hold. Your life wasn't meant to be boring. Your life isn't necessarily meant to follow one old strict program. Remember, a moving man is a winning man. Cheers to your becoming.
